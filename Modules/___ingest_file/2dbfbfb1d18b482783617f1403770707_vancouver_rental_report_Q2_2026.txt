Vancouver Rental Market Report - Q2 2026
Prepared by: BC Housing Market Analytics Group
Date: June 30, 2026

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The Vancouver rental market continued to show strong demand in Q2 2026, 
with average monthly rents increasing 4.2% year-over-year. 
Vacancy rates remain historically low at 1.3%, driven by population 
growth and limited new supply entering the market.

KEY FINDINGS

1. Average Monthly Rents by Bedroom Type:
   - Studio/Bachelor: $1,950 (+3.1% YoY)
   - 1 Bedroom: $2,650 (+4.5% YoY)
   - 2 Bedroom: $3,400 (+4.8% YoY)
   - 3 Bedroom: $4,800 (+5.2% YoY)

2. Top Neighbourhoods by Average Rent:
   - Coal Harbour: $5,200/month
   - Yaletown: $4,750/month
   - West End: $3,960/month
   - Kitsilano: $4,400/month
   - Mount Pleasant: $3,200/month

3. Market Demand Indicators:
   - Average days on market: 6.2 days (down from 8.1 in Q1)
   - Multiple applications per listing: 73% of listings
   - Listings below asking price: less than 5%

4. Supply Constraints:
   - New rental units completed Q2 2026: 1,240 units
   - Units under construction: 8,500 units (expected completion 2027-2028)
   - Purpose-built rental starts: down 12% vs Q2 2025

REGIONAL BREAKDOWN

Downtown Core:
Average rent $4,100/month. High demand from tech workers and 
international students. Limited vacancy in luxury segment.

East Vancouver:
Average rent $2,800/month. Growing popularity among young professionals.
Commercial Drive and Mount Pleasant seeing highest demand.

North Vancouver:
Average rent $3,100/month. Family-oriented market. 
3-bedroom units have less than 0.8% vacancy.

Burnaby/New Westminster:
Average rent $2,600/month. Transit-oriented development driving demand
near SkyTrain stations. Metrotown area seeing 15% YoY rent growth.

FORECAST Q3 2026

Rents expected to increase a further 2-3% in Q3 2026. 
Demand will remain elevated due to:
- UBC and SFU fall semester intake (estimated 12,000 new students)
- Continued interprovincial migration to BC
- Limited new supply expected before Q4 2026

RISK FACTORS
- Interest rate changes affecting investor landlords
- Potential provincial rent control policy updates
- Economic slowdown reducing immigration targets
